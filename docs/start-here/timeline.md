# T2 Regulation timeline

!!! warning "Not legal advice"
    This page lists dates from the law and from official Commission pages.
    It does not tell you whether a rule applies to you. Check the source
    and ask your competent authority if you are not sure.

## In one sentence

One page with the key dates for CSRD/ESRS, VSME and CBAM, so you can plan
your data work backwards from each deadline.

## Who does this and when

The sustainability lead, finance lead or adviser who plans reporting work.
Read it at the start of each year and after every regulatory change. The
"Last checked" date at the top tells you how fresh the dates are.

## Inputs

- Your financial year (calendar year or other).
- Which regimes may apply to you. If you do not know, start with
  [T1 Which regime applies to me?](triage.md).

## Steps

1. Find your regime in the tables below.
2. For each date, write down what you must have ready by then.
3. Count back from the date. Supplier data and audits often take months.
4. Put the dates in your team calendar with a reminder well before each one.
5. Check this page again when the "Last checked" date is more than three
   months old. Rules are still changing.

### VSME (voluntary standard)

| Date | What happens | Who it affects | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Jul 2026 | Commission adopts the voluntary sustainability reporting standard for smaller companies as a delegated act, together with the revised ESRS | SMEs outside CSRD scope that get ESG data requests from customers, banks or investors | [European Commission](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-adopts-revised-sustainability-reporting-standards-2026-07-03_en) |

VSME is voluntary. There is no filing deadline. Your deadline is the date
your customer or bank asks for the data.

### CBAM

| Date | What happens | Who it affects | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan 2026 | Definitive period starts. Each calendar year is one reporting period | EU importers of CBAM goods and their customs representatives | [DG TAXUD](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en) |
| From 1 Jan 2026 | Threshold: importers with **50 tonnes net mass or less per year** (all CN codes added together) are exempt. The exemption does not cover hydrogen or electricity | All importers of CBAM goods | [Regulation (EU) 2025/2083](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2083/oj/eng) |

| 1 Jan 2027 | Certificate price becomes a weekly average of EU ETS auction prices | Authorised CBAM declarants | [DG TAXUD](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/price-cbam-certificates_en) |

### Scope 3

Scope 3 has no legal deadline of its own. It feeds ESRS E1 (climate) and
VSME, so it follows their dates, and the dates your customers set in
their supplier questionnaires.

## Worked example

Melita Metals Ltd is a fictional importer in Malta. It brings in steel bars
from outside the EU, well above the 50-tonne threshold each year.

- **CBAM.** Its 2026 imports fall in the first reporting period. It plans
  to collect emissions data from its suppliers during 2026, buy
  certificates from February 2027, and file the declaration and surrender
  certificates by 30 September 2027. From 2027 it also checks its
  certificate balance at the end of every quarter against the 50% rule.
- **VSME.** A bank asks for ESG data with the loan renewal. Melita Metals
  uses the voluntary standard to answer, and sets the bank's date as its
  deadline.
- **CSRD.** Melita Metals checks the 1,000-employee and EUR 450 m turnover
  test against the source. It then confirms the result with its adviser,
  because Malta's transposition is still to come.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for 30 September 2027 to start. Supplier emissions data and
  certificate purchases take months.
- Forgetting the quarterly 50% holding rule from 2027. It means buying
  certificates during the year, not only at the end.
- Assuming the wave 1 exemption for FY2025 and FY2026 applies everywhere.
  Each Member State decides.
- Using a date from a news article or blog. Always check the official
  source; dates move.
