# 1. Base year

Our base year is **[year]**, our first complete greenhouse gas inventory
covering Scope 1, 2 and 3. Targets and progress are measured against it.

# 2. What triggers a recalculation

We will consider recalculating the base year when we find:

| ☐ | Type of change | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Structural change | Acquisition or sale of a company or site; outsourcing or insourcing of an activity |
| ☐ | Change in calculation method or data source | A new emission factor or factor source; supplier data replacing an estimate; a new allocation method |
| ☐ | Error | A mistake in data or calculation, including several small errors that are significant together |

Changes caused only by real changes in activity (we bought more or less,
or a supplier cut its emissions) are **not** recalculated: they are what
the inventory should show.

# 3. Significance threshold

We recalculate when the changes found in a year add up to **[5]%** or more
of the base-year total of the scope concerned.

We add up the **absolute** size of each change, so that changes in
opposite directions do not cancel each other out.

We disclose this threshold with our inventory.

# 4. How we recalculate

- **Structural changes** are applied to the whole base year, as if the
  change had always been in place, whatever the date of the change.
- **Method or data-source changes** are applied by recalculating the base
  year with the new method.
- **Errors** are corrected in the base year and in every later year
  affected.

# 5. Disclosure

Each year we state what changes were found, their size, the result of the
test and, if the base year was recalculated, the old and new base-year
totals.

# 6. Review

We review this policy every year with the inventory, and when the GHG
Protocol's base-year rules change.
